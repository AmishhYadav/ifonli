## Customer accounts, order actions and DPDP (Phase 5, 2026-10-31, plan-resolved/reversible)

| ID | Decision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| D-ACC-1 | Admin sessions in the customer area | An admin-role session (owner/admin/fulfilment) is not a customer. `requireCustomer()` sends it to /admin (or throws `ForbiddenError` in Server Actions); OTP login is refused for admin addresses and the login screen never reveals which addresses are staff. |
| D-ACC-2 | Which orders an account sees | Orders with `user_id` = the account, plus orders whose e-mail equals the account's VERIFIED e-mail (guest orders placed before signing up). An unverified e-mail grants nothing by address. Expired (never paid) checkouts are left out of the history. |
| D-ACC-3 | Customer cancel | Allowed while the fulfilment status is in `cancellable_statuses`, but never past `packed` whatever the setting says (a shipped parcel cannot be recalled). Prepaid: automatic full refund; COD: cancel only. Same transaction as the admin cancel (`cancelOrderCore`). Idempotent; rate limited by IP and order. |
| D-ACC-4 | Claim window for "Report a problem" | `damaged_claim_window_hours` never blocks a report (statutory rights for defective/damaged/wrong goods). A report after the window is accepted, flagged in the case notes and the team alert ("[Late report]"), and the customer is told kindly. A placeholder (unset) window flags nothing. Goods problems are offered once the parcel is shipped; "Something else" any time the order is live. |
| D-ACC-5 | Support photos | Up to 4 JPEG/PNG/WebP, 8 MB each, uploaded straight to the private bucket with signed upload URLs (the order must be owned; rate limited); the case only stores paths of files that really exist with an image type and size. Without Supabase Storage configured the picker is hidden and the report still works. EXIF is not stripped (the bucket is private and only staff read it). |
| D-ACC-6 | Wishlist sync | First sign-in from a device: union of device and account lists (earliest save time wins), pushed back. Afterwards the account is the source of truth and the device follows it, so a removal on another device sticks. Sign-out clears the device copy. A non-personal hint cookie (`ifonli_acct`) tells the browser whether to run the sync; the Server Actions re-check the real session. |
| D-DPDP-1 | Account deletion and retention | Deleted: login (user, sessions, accounts), profile, saved addresses, wishlist, open carts, pending sign-in codes, **every notification-outbox row** (addressed to the customer or about their orders), **contact-form messages** from their address, and the **photos of their support cases** (private bucket, removed after the database commit; a storage failure is logged and does not undo the erasure). Anonymised: the free text of their support cases becomes "[Removed at the customer's request]" (the case, its type, status, resolution and staff notes stay with the order), and the e-mail on their coupon redemptions becomes `deleted-<id>@deleted.invalid` (the redemption still counts against the coupon's limits). Kept: orders, lines, events, shipments, invoices and refunds, with the name/address/e-mail/phone typed at checkout (tax and accounting records, consumer-protection evidence); `orders.user_id` becomes NULL so they belong to no account. "Their orders" = linked to the account or placed with its verified e-mail. An audit row (`customer.delete`) records counts only, never the e-mail. A person who signs up again with the same e-mail sees those orders again (they match by verified e-mail). Export (`/api/account/export`) covers profile, addresses, orders, visible timeline, shipments, wishlist, support cases (no staff notes), contact messages, **coupon redemptions and the e-mails sent to them**. Retention in the daily cleanup: delivered e-mails (`notification_outbox` status `sent`) are deleted after 90 days; expired Better Auth sessions and verification rows are deleted. |

## Admin customers (Phase 6, 2026-10-31, plan-resolved/reversible)

| ID | Decision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| D-CUS-1 | Who is a customer | One person per lower-cased e-mail: an account (`user.role = customer`) or a guest (an e-mail with at least one real order and no `user` row). Staff roles never appear. Orders belong to the account they are linked to, else to whoever owns the order e-mail (D-ACC-2). Checkouts that never completed (`pending_payment`, `expired`) do not make anyone a customer. No customer table: the list is one grouped query over `orders` joined to `user`. |
| D-CUS-2 | Order count and lifetime value | The dashboard's counted sale: confirmed/processing/completed, payment not refunded or failed, no `replacement:` orders. A partially refunded order counts in full, as on the dashboard. |
| D-CUS-3 | Customer URLs | An account is its user id; a guest is `o-<order id>` (any of their orders), so no e-mail address sits in a URL. A guest link whose e-mail later became an account redirects to the account. |
| D-CUS-4 | DPDP requests in admin | Export reuses `exportCustomerData` (accounts) and `exportGuestData` (guests: orders, cases, messages). Delete reuses `deleteCustomerAccount` after the admin types the customer's e-mail; guests are refused with an explanation (orders are retained records, D-DPDP-1). Both write audit rows (`customer.export`, `customer.delete`) with counts only. Permissions `customer:export` and `customer:delete` are owner and admin only; fulfilment has no customer access. |

## Security and operations decisions (Phase 8, 2026-10-31, plan-resolved/reversible)

| ID | Decision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| D-SEC-1 | CSP strategy: nonce vs hash vs static | **Static policy from `next.config.ts` headers** (built in `src/lib/security/headers.ts`), `script-src 'self' 'unsafe-inline'` plus the fixed Razorpay and Google Tag Manager / Analytics origins (Google always, not only when `NEXT_PUBLIC_GA4_ID` is set: the GA4 id may come from the admin setting, which the build cannot see; gtag still loads only after an id exists and consent is given), no `unsafe-eval` in production. Trade-off: a per-request nonce (the Next.js recommended strict CSP) needs dynamic rendering for every page, which turns off static shells, PPR and CDN caching, and the PPR static shell's inline bootstrap scripts could not carry a nonce anyway. The experimental SRI mode only hashes external files; Next's inline flight-data chunks and our no-flash `VibeHeadScript` stay inline. So inline script execution is allowed and the policy compensates with: `object-src 'none'`, `base-uri 'self'`, `form-action 'self'`, `frame-ancestors 'none'`, a short `connect-src`/`frame-src`/`img-src` allow-list, React output escaping and JSON-LD built from trusted data. Revisit when Next supports hash/nonce for static shells |
| D-SEC-2 | CSP rollout mode | `CSP_MODE=report-only` (default) enforces only a baseline (`frame-ancestors`, `object-src`, `base-uri`) and sends the full policy as `Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only`; `CSP_MODE=enforce` enforces everything. Violations go to `POST /api/csp-report` (rate limited, compact log line). The value is read at **build** time (headers are baked into the routes manifest): change it in Vercel and redeploy. Flip to `enforce` after a live Razorpay test payment shows no reports. CI builds with `enforce` |
| D-SEC-3 | Cross-Origin-Opener-Policy | `same-origin-allow-popups` (keeps `window.opener` for Razorpay redirect/3-D Secure popups; `same-origin` would break them). No COEP/CORP |
| D-SEC-4 | Zod in the browser | `src/lib/zod-csp.ts` switches Zod to the interpreter (`jitless`) in the browser. Zod's JIT probe (`new Function`) is reported as a CSP violation even though it is caught; the three client-reachable schema modules import it first. The server keeps the JIT |
| D-SEC-5 | Better Auth rate-limit store | Better Auth's default store is in memory (per serverless instance, ineffective on Vercel). Its `customStorage.consume` now calls the app's Postgres limiter (`rate_limits`, keys prefixed `ba:`). Added a per-email cap on sign-in codes (5 per 10 minutes) on top of the per-IP rules. **Update (deep review):** the per-email cap is now only on *sending* codes (5 per 10 min per email, plus 20 per 10 min per IP). Entering a code has its own budget that counts **failed** attempts only, per (email, IP) and per IP, so sending codes cannot use it up and a stranger cannot lock the real customer out. |
| D-OPS-1 | Error monitoring | **Dependency-free reporter** speaking Sentry's envelope API (`src/lib/monitoring`), not `@sentry/nextjs`: the SDK wraps `next.config`, patches the request pipeline (friction with Cache Components prerendering) and adds roughly 40 KB gzip to the client against a 150 KB budget, while we only need "tell me when it throws, without PII". No-op without `SENTRY_DSN`; `release` = `VERCEL_GIT_COMMIT_SHA`; events carry no request bodies, headers, cookies or user; messages, URLs and tags are scrubbed (emails, phones, `?t=` tokens, bearer tokens, long opaque ids, pincodes, bound SQL params). Browser errors go through the same-origin `/api/monitoring` tunnel (DSN stays server-side, no CSP change). Source maps are not uploaded: add them (or switch to the SDK) if readable minified client stacks become necessary |
| D-OPS-2 | Health endpoint | `GET /api/health`: `select 1` with a 2 s timeout, memoised for 5 s per instance, `no-store`, returns status + DB latency + short release id only |

## Money path and server-side cart review fixes (2026-10-31, plan-resolved/reversible)

| ID | Decision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| D-PAY-1 | Retrying a checkout whose order died unpaid | If the order found by the idempotency key is `expired`, or `cancelled` without ever being paid, the same submit is a **fresh attempt** under the next key (`<key>~1`, `~2`, ...); a double click on a live attempt still replays it. A paid-then-cancelled order is never replayed (`checkout_expired`). The checkout form also drops its stored key after `payment_init_failed` / `checkout_expired` |
| D-PAY-2 | Own reservation vs own cart | Cart reconciliation (view, add, coupon) gives back the units held by the cart's own live `pending_payment` orders, so dismissing the payment window never cuts the cart. The next checkout supersedes those orders anyway. Other carts still see the stock as held |
| D-PAY-3 | Late capture and coupon limits | A late capture still keeps the sale and restores the released redemption (the customer paid the discounted price), but under the coupon row lock the usage and per-customer limits are re-checked. If they are already used up the redemption is confirmed anyway (so counts show the overshoot), an internal `coupon_over_limit` order event is written and the admin gets a `payment_anomaly` alert ("Coupon limit exceeded by a late payment") |
| D-PAY-4 | Amount mismatch after capture | Not confirmed; the whole captured amount is refunded automatically (same path as the no-stock refund, disposition `order_closed`, refund reason names the mismatch) and the admin is alerted. Replays of the same payment do not refund twice |
| D-PAY-5 | Guest order token on a replayed submit | The raw token cannot be recovered from its hash, so a replay mints a new one and ADDS its hash to `orders.access_token_hash` (newest 5, space-separated, `commerce/order-token.ts`). Earlier links and payment windows keep working. Every verifier (`orders`, `customer-orders`, payments verify / retry) uses `orderTokenMatches` |
| D-PAY-6 | Cart clearing on capture | A late capture clears the cart only if no newer order exists for it and its lines are still exactly the order's lines |
| D-PAY-7 | Reconcile late-capture sweep | Candidates are expired unpaid prepaid orders from the last 6 hours; at most `limit` are polled per run, starting at a random place in the list and wrapping, so no order is starved by 50 older ones |
| D-PAY-8 | COD cap | `cod_max_order_value` is compared with the total the customer pays, COD fee included (plan 7.9 `total <= cod_max_order_value`). Goods plus shipping may therefore be at most the cap minus the fee |

Only blockers are computed (no content warnings) and the answer is cached 60 s per server instance, so a fixed checklist opens the shop within a minute. There is no owner override (plan 14 mentions one for soft launches; not built). CSP not in `enforce` mode and the review-pages flag are launch blockers too |
| D-PAY-10 | `ORDER_TOKEN_SECRET` | Removed from the launch checks, `.env.example`, README and RUNBOOK: order tokens are random and stored as hashes, nothing signs with a secret. The optional declaration in `src/lib/env.ts` is left for its owner to delete |

| D-ADM-2 | Dispatching refunded orders | Packing or shipping is refused for a fully refunded order and for a prepaid order whose payment is not `paid`. A **partially** refunded order can still ship (a goodwill refund of the shipping fee is a live sale) |

## Security hardening (deep review, 2026-10-31, reversible)

| ID | Decision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| D-SEC-6 | Better Auth over HTTP | The app never uses Better Auth's browser client: all sign-in, OTP, two-factor and account actions are Server Actions calling `auth.api.*` in-process. So `/api/auth/*` answers only `get-session`, `sign-out`, `ok` and `error`; everything else is a uniform 404 (the `before` hook refuses any call that arrives with a `Request`). That removes the staff-email probe (403 vs 200 on the OTP send), HTTP guessing of passwords/codes/TOTP outside our limits, and the admin plugin's `/admin/*` endpoints (set-role, ban, remove, set-password, impersonate) which would bypass `admin_audit_log` and the last-owner / not-self rules in `services/admin/users.ts`. Revisit if a browser client is ever introduced |
| D-SEC-7 | Temporary staff passwords | A password an owner sets or resets, or a 2FA reset, marks the account (`user.password_change_deadline`, NULL when nothing is pending; it was a `verification` row until migration 0005, D-DB-4). While enrolling TOTP the person must choose a new password (`changePassword` with `revokeOtherSessions`, so a first-comer who knew the temporary password loses their session), and the temporary password stops signing in after 7 days. Only accounts not yet enrolled are marked, so no active admin can be locked out |
| D-SEC-8 | Uploads | Product images are re-encoded with `sharp` after validation (same format, orientation applied to the pixels, EXIF/GPS/XMP dropped, sRGB) and the cleaned copy replaces the upload; an upload that cannot be cleaned is deleted. Support photos are checked by their first bytes (JPEG, PNG, WebP) against the claimed type and the extension in the path, not just the Content-Type |
| D-SEC-9 | Boot-time environment check | A production server (not `next build`) fails at startup with a list of missing or weak variables (`assertProductionEnv`, called from `instrumentation-node.ts`): site URL (https, or localhost for local production runs), `BETTER_AUTH_SECRET` and `CRON_SECRET` (32+ characters), database, Resend, Razorpay. `ORDER_TOKEN_SECRET` is not part of it |
| D-OPS-3 | Function region and database pool | `vercel.json` pins `regions: ["bom1"]` next to the Supabase database. The postgres.js client uses `max: 5`, `idle_timeout: 20`, `connect_timeout: 10` and TLS for any non-local host |

The low-stock digest is queued at most once per India day |
| D-OPS-5 | Seed and admin scripts | `tsx` leaves `NODE_ENV` unset, so the scripts decide by the host in `DATABASE_URL`: not localhost/127.0.0.1 needs `--allow-remote` (`seed-products` also `--force-placeholder`), and `seed` needs `SEED_OWNER_EMAIL`. ## Database hardening (deep review F4, 2026-11-01, reversible)

Migration `0005_db_hardening.sql`. Run it before the first staging migrate is exposed to the internet (RUNBOOK section 3).

| ID | Decision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| D-DB-1 | RLS lockdown | Hosted Supabase's default ACL grants `anon` and `authenticated` full CRUD on every new table in `public`, reachable through the Data API with the public anon key. The app never uses that API (it connects as the table owner `postgres`, which RLS does not apply to), so every table has RLS enabled with no policies (deny-all; declared with `.enableRLS()` so Drizzle snapshots match) and both roles lose every privilege on tables, sequences and functions in `public`, plus `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES FOR ROLE postgres` so tables added later do not get them back. Each revoke is guarded by a `pg_roles` check because CI and the integration tests use plain Postgres; the test `globalSetup` creates the roles and the hosted default grants so the guarded branch really runs. `service_role` is left alone: it is the server-side key (bypasses RLS), never shipped to a browser, and Supabase's own tooling expects it. New tables must use `.enableRLS()`; `tests/integration/db-hardening.test.ts` fails otherwise. `PUBLIC` keeps its default EXECUTE on functions (only a pure helper, `immutable_unaccent`, is affected) |
| D-DB-2 | `admin_audit_log` is append-only | A row trigger (`admin_audit_log_guard`) rejects DELETE and every UPDATE except one: `actor_id` going from a value to NULL with all other columns unchanged. That is exactly what the existing `ON DELETE SET NULL` foreign key does when a staff or customer account is deleted, so DPDP erasure (D-DPDP-1) and staff removal keep working and the row stays as evidence with no actor. Nothing in `src/` or `scripts/` updated or deleted audit rows. The only deleter was the e2e helper `cleanupQaAdmins`, which now deletes just the QA users and leaves their audit rows (dev databases accumulate a few anonymous rows). TRUNCATE is not blocked (row triggers do not fire on it): the integration suites reset with it, and it needs table-owner rights, which no API role has |
| D-DB-3 | Payment and reservation indexes | `payments`: partial unique index on `order_id` where `status = 'captured'` and `raw.disposition` is NULL, i.e. at most one payment per order is the one that PAID it. A plain "one captured per order" would break legitimate flows: a second charge, a capture after the stock sold out, a capture on a closed order and an amount mismatch are all stored as `captured` rows (stamped with `raw.disposition` by `refuseCapture`) and auto-refunded. `stock_reservations`: unique (`order_id`, `variant_id`), replacing the order-only index. Safe because `createFromCart` inserts the rows once, one per cart line (`cart_items` is unique per variant, `priceCart` maps lines 1:1) and later code only changes `status` in place |
| D-DB-4 | Must-change-password column | `user.password_change_deadline` (timestamptz, NULL = nothing pending) replaces the `must-change-password:<userId>` row in `verification`. Same functions in `src/server/auth/admin-user.ts`. Better Auth ignores the extra column (it is not an `additionalField`). The migration copies existing deadlines (an unreadable value becomes the epoch, i.e. expired, as before) and deletes the old rows. The daily verification sweep can no longer touch the marker, so the far-future `expires_at` trick is gone |
| D-DB-5 | Product slug redirects | Changing a product's slug records the old one in `product_slug_redirects (old_slug PK, product_id -> products ON DELETE CASCADE)`. It points at the product, so a chain a -> b -> c needs no upkeep: both old slugs resolve to the product's current slug. A live product slug always wins: creating, renaming or duplicating a product onto a redirect's slug deletes that redirect, and the PDP only looks a redirect up after `getProductBySlug` found nothing. The PDP answers with `permanentRedirect` (HTTP 308, the permanent redirect Next.js emits; it passes link equity like a 301) to `/products/{current slug}`, only when the product is active and its type active (otherwise a normal 404). The lookup is `'use cache'`d with the old slug tag and the product tag, both of which the admin update already expires |

## Retention and payment-failed e-mail (2026-11-01, reversible)

| ID | Decision | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| D-RET-1 | Personal-data retention in the daily cleanup | `services/retention.ts`, run by `/api/cron/cleanup`, each step capped at 500 rows per run. **Contact messages** with status `closed` are deleted 365 days after their last change (`new` and `open` ones are never auto-deleted: they still need an answer). **Support cases** that are `resolved` or `rejected` for 365 days: the description becomes "[Removed after the retention period]" and the photos are deleted from the private bucket; the case row, type, status, resolution, staff notes and the link to the order stay (the order holds the contact details and is the accounting record).
